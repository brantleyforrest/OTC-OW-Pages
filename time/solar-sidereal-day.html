The difference always equals the number of orbits completed. On real Earth, one year contains 366.24 sidereal days and 365.24 solar days. To see the pattern quickly, set the exaggeration to its maximum, turn off the milestone pauses, and let one full orbit play.

Why the Difference Matters
  • Geostationary orbits must match the sidereal day, because that is Earth's true rotation period.
  • Stars rise about 4 minutes earlier each night, or about 2 hours earlier each month.
  • Civil clocks follow the solar day, because daily life follows the Sun.
