10 Rotations, Step by Step
  1. Start of day 1The marked point faces the Sun, and a distant star lies in the same direction.
  2. 10 sidereal days: 10 full rotations (3,600°)Earth completes 10 full 360° spins facing the distant star. Along its orbit, Earth has traveled 9.9°, falling behind the Sun.
  3. 10 solar days: 10 full days (240 h)After an accumulated 9.9° extra rotation (39 m), the point faces the Sun again.

The difference always equals the number of orbits completed. A real year has 366.24 sidereal days and 365.24 solar days. Over any period, the sidereal day count minus the solar day count exactly equals the fraction of the orbit completed.

Why the Difference Matters
  • Geostationary orbits must match the sidereal day, because that is Earth's true rotation period.
  • Stars rise about 4 minutes earlier each night, or about 2 hours earlier each month.
  • Civil clocks follow the solar day, because daily life follows the Sun.
